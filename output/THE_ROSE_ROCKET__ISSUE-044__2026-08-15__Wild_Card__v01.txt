  THE BIG SHIFT IN ONE SENTENCE
  Most AI products ask you to leave the
  room and enter a screen. Augmented
  reality does the opposite: it leaves
  the intelligence in the room and asks
  the room to become more interesting
  than the pain.

  WHAT "AUGMENTED" ACTUALLY MEANS
  Not a headset city. Not a metaverse
  pitch. A phone camera pointed at a
  card, a decal, a wall, a stretch of
  floor. Software recognizes a marker
  or a space, then draws a creature,
  a treasure, a tiny story on top of
  what is already there.

  The physical world stays load-bearing.
  The digital layer is the invitation.

  WHY SATURDAY GETS THIS STORY
  Wednesday was chips. Thursday would
  have been image machines. Friday was
  builders. Saturday is the lab: one
  company, one thesis, enough pages to
  see the joints.

  ALTality is not a household name.
  SpellBound is the name on the door
  hospitals recognize. The legal entity
  and the product brand are the same
  bet wearing two badges. That split
  is part of the lesson.

  THE THESIS
  If you want a child to move, laugh,
  or sit still for a needle, do not
  lecture them about outcomes. Change
  the scene they are standing in.

  Child-life teams already knew this.
  ALTality's move was to put the scene
  change in a staff pocket: a phone,
  a tablet, a TV, a printed trigger.
  No cart the size of a vending machine.
  No "when the specialist is free."

  THREE LAYERS OF THE STACK

  1) DISTRACTION
     Short procedures. Shots. IVs.
     A nurse once said the shots were
     the easiest she had ever done for
     a five-year-old. That is not a
     feature list. That is a room that
     stopped being only a procedure.

  2) MOBILITY
     After surgery, the medical advice
     is often brutal and simple: get
     up. Walk. The body heals faster
     when it is not a statue. Kids do
     not take that briefing the way
     adults pretend to. ARISE turns
     the recovery space into a hunt.

  3) EDUCATION
     SpellBound's longer arc is not
     only "make the wait less ugly."
     It is: teach the next step of
     care in a form a child can hold.
     Knowledge as a tool, not a pamphlet
     that dies in a folder.

  THE LOOP (PLAIN LANGUAGE)
  1. Care team hands over a device or
     points a device at a marker.
  2. The app locks onto the room.
  3. A story starts -- often underwater
     in ARISE: creatures, treasure,
     targets that only resolve if you
     move.
  4. The child walks to play.
  5. Walking is the therapy. Play is
     the delivery system.
  6. Staff watch distance, mood, and
     whether the next dose of extra
     comfort medicine is still needed.

  That last step is why federal research
  money showed up. Not because AR is
  cute. Because movement and attention
  are medical variables.

  LIMITS WORTH RESPECTING
  - A game is not a surgeon. Nobody
    serious claims otherwise.
  - Hospitals are messy RF environments.
    Wi-Fi, infection rules, shared
    devices, and staff time will kill
    a pretty demo that cannot survive
    a Tuesday swing shift.
  - Kids share games. That is a feature
    when they recruit the next room.
    It is a problem if the device is
    the scarce object.
  - Research takes years. A press page
    is not a finished trial.

  FIELD NOTE FOR MARKO
  If you only keep one Saturday idea:
  the interesting AI is not always the
  one that talks. Sometimes it is the
  one that makes a hallway mean
  something, so a person will use their
  own legs.

  idea walked into a children's

- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  THE PIVOT: FROM BOOKSTORE TO WARD
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

  Hospitals already run on paper,
  stickers, badges, walls, and wait.
  That is hostile terrain for a kid
  and friendly terrain for marker-
  based AR.

  SpellBound's pitch to care teams
  is blunt and unromantic:

  - waiting rooms do not have to be
    a punishment chamber
  - a shot does not have to be the
    whole story of the visit
  - recovery does not have to be
    a bed and a clock
  - education should survive the
    ride home

  "Care never happens in a vacuum"
  is their own line. One bad hour
  poisons the next hour. One good
  hour can buy compliance for the
  thing that actually heals.

  Child-life specialists are the
  native users. Nurses are the
  stress test. Parents are the
  audience that decides whether
  the tool was magic or a toy
  that got in the way.

- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

  ARISE
  Augmented Reality Incorporating
  Spatial Enhancement. A scavenger-
  hunt game for inpatient recovery.
  Underwater story. Creatures.
  Treasure. Targets in the room.
  The point is independent play that
  happens to be walking.

  Staff quotes on the public record:
  kids who got ARISE started telling
  other kids to walk by their rooms.
  People began "prescribing" it as
  movement therapy. That is product-
  market fit spoken in hospital
  English, not pitch-deck English.

  QUICK DISTRACTIONS
  Point-of-care tools for injections
  and short procedures. Cards or
  decals trigger a 3D bit of theater
  long enough for a needle to become
  a side plot.

  SPELLBOUND ARCADE
  A library of AR games for waiting
  rooms and bedside: hospital TVs,
  tablets, phones. Kill the dead air
  without turning the clinic into a
  casino.

  PROCEDURE SIMULATORS
  Child-friendly walkthroughs of
  what is about to happen. Fear
  likes a blank. A rehearsal fills
  the blank with a map.

  AUGMENTED BOOKS
  The origin product, still in the
  kit: paper plus camera, characters
  that stand up off the page.

  SPACE / WAITING-ROOM LAYERS
  Custom room and wall experiences.
  The architecture becomes a stage
  instead of a corridor with bad
  art and a ticking clock.


- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  THE RESEARCH BET
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

  Cute is not a grant strategy.
  ALTality went after a harder
  claim: after major surgery, some
  children with cancer need a lot
  of pain medicine. Some of that
  need is tissue. Some of it is
  attention, fear, and stillness.

  If a game can get a kid moving
  and looking at something other
  than the hurt, maybe the extra
  medicine curve bends. Maybe the
  stay shortens. Maybe the long
  tail of dependence gets a little
  less likely. Those are research
  questions, not slogans.

  What is on the public record:

  * Legal sponsor: ALTality, Inc.
    (SpellBound). PI / contact in
    early STTR materials: Christina
    York, Ann Arbor.

  * Game under study: ARISE.

  * Clinical partners named in the
    federal writeup: pediatric
    oncology, anesthesia, and pain
    teams at MD Anderson Cancer
    Center and Baylor / Texas
    Children's Hospital.

  * Phase I job: prove you can even
    study this on an inpatient
    postoperative ward without the
    protocol falling apart.

  * Phase II ambition (as written):
    a multi-site randomized trial
    nicknamed AURORA -- Augmented
    Reality for Opioid Reduction in
    Childhood Cancer.

  * 2020 newsroom: a $1.8 million
    NIDA fast-track award to study
    ARISE against length of stay
    and opioid use after surgery.

  * Later federal slices include
    feasibility work on a dual
    English / Spanish mobile AR
    pain-assessment app for
    Hispanic / Latino pediatric
    and adolescent cancer patients.

  Read that stack the way a builder
  reads it: they did not only ship
  a game. They tried to put the
  game on a scoreboard medicine
  already knows how to read --
  doses, days, movement.

  Honest caveat: a funded study is
  not the same thing as a finished
  proof. Saturday respects the
  difference. The ambition is still
  the story.


- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
  HOW ARISE PLAYS (NO HEADSET MYTH)
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

  Forget the visor commercial.
  ARISE is a scavenger hunt on a
  phone or tablet the staff already
  understand.

  The recovery space is the board.
  Targets live in the room. The
  story is underwater: find, play,
  collect, gather. Each objective
  is an excuse to stand, turn,
  walk, reach.

  That matters for three unromantic
  reasons:

  1. Early mobility is not a vibe.
     Lungs, guts, clots, mood --
     hospitals already preach it.
  2. Kids recruit kids. A game that
     leaks into the hallway is a
     distribution strategy.
  3. Staff can point at a behavior
     ("go play") instead of a
     lecture ("you should ambulate").

  The best products steal an existing
  verb. ARISE steals "go look."
